03 · Fair play

Provably fair loot with commit–reveal

1 · COMMIT

Before the match the server publishes SHA‑256(seed). It can’t change the seed later.

2 · ROLL

Each drop = HMAC(seed, client seed + round), so nothing is pre‑picked.

3 · REVEAL

Afterwards the seed is published. Anyone re‑hashes it and re‑rolls every drop.

PEER‑TO‑PEER VERSION

Each player commits, then all reveal; the XOR is the roll. No reveal = forfeit.

Cryptography in Online Multiplayer Games · 10

Beyond the basics · Network privacy

Relays keep player IPs secret

THE PROBLEM

A direct connection shows every peer your IP address, an easy target for a flood that ends the match.

VALVE’S STEAM DATAGRAM RELAY

Traffic goes through relays, so IP addresses are never revealed. Everything received is authenticated, encrypted and rate‑limited.

CRYPTO DOES THE GATEKEEPING

Relay tickets let one client reach one server for a limited time; players hold short‑term certificates.

Beyond the basics · The quantum future

Getting ready for quantum computers

THE THREAT · HARVEST NOW, DECRYPT LATER

Traffic recorded today can be opened once a large quantum computer breaks X25519, ECDSA and RSA.

AES‑256 and SHA‑256 stay safe. Only key exchange and signatures need replacing; logins, payments and chat matter more than match traffic.
